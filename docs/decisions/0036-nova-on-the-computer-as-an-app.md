# 0036: Nova on the computer as an app

20 November 2026 · Asked for by the user in chat; the menu approved as drawn ("yes that looks good") and the icon chosen from four · Design: [the spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-pc-app-design.md) · Built; his named deploys and hand checks (71 to 95) to come

**Context.** What Nova does on the PC is basically finished (his words): push-to-talk (0026), Windows' media controls (0031), opening things and PC commands (0034), the microphone's mute and the orb on the screen (0035). What was left was polish. Nova on the PC was a windowless `pythonw` started by a logon task. It had nothing in the system tray and no way to quit or restart it by hand. The volume mixer and Task Manager said "Python" with Python's icon. The Start-menu "Nova" did nothing while it ran. The key that makes it listen lived in his own AutoHotkey script, not in Nova. He wanted it to look and behave like an app of its own, as one of the last pieces for the PC: fingerprint approval comes after and will live in this app, then the Mac version is made to the same shape.

**What he decided.**

1. **The menu**, as drawn: Listen (with its shortcut beside it), Play / Pause, Next, Mute microphone (ticked while muted), a line, Settings, Start with Windows (ticked while on), Restart Nova, Quit.
2. **One icon, named "Nova"**, wherever Windows shows it: the tray, the Start menu, the volume mixer, the media pop-up, Task Manager and the "is using your microphone" notice.
3. **The icon is the orb as it is on the screen** (option A of four shown): the five lobes and the glow, still and small. He was told it is the weakest of the four when tiny, and that is why the small sizes are drawn tighter (below).
4. **A click on the icon, left or right, opens the menu.**
5. **A shortcut of Nova's own** makes it listen: Ctrl+Alt+N until he changes it on the settings page ("a default one which can be changed"). Numpad 3 goes on working exactly as before.
6. **No window.** The tray icon, the menu and the orb are the whole app.

The rest was left to me and is his to overturn.

**What was decided, and why.**

- **The app grows out of the satellite (approach 1).** The tray icon, menu and shortcut are made through Windows' own calls from Python (ctypes), as the orb's window is, and a small `Nova.exe` runs the satellite so Windows names the process Nova. The others, and why not:
  - *Ready-made tray and hotkey libraries* (pystray, pynput, keyboard): less code for two pieces, but new packages (pystray needs Pillow), less say over the menu, a fight for the main thread on the Mac, and none of them names the process, so most of this would remain.
  - *Bundling Nova as an installed program* (PyInstaller and an installer): it would be in the installed apps list, but every update would be built on Windows, deploys get heavier, unsigned bundles trip antivirus, and he would see little of it day to day. Worth it only if Nova is ever given to someone else.
  - *A separate native app wrapped round the satellite*: two codebases on each system.
- **The tray lives in the satellite, not the launcher.** The satellite stays up and keeps trying when the brain is away, so the grey icon covers that. Only when it can't start at all (no password yet, a broken install) is there no icon, as there was none before, and the reason is in the launcher's log. The icon is gone for about two seconds over a Restart. A launcher with an icon of its own would need the satellite's state passed between two processes for a case that rare.
- **The tray has a thread of its own** (`voice_assistant/tray.py`), which owns a hidden window and waits on Windows' messages, so a click or the shortcut is acted on at once. The satellite only tells it things, and every call returns at once, as the orb's do: a press, the chime, a reply or music never waits on the tray, and a tray which can't be made is logged once and Nova carries on as before, Numpad 3 included. The icon is put back when Explorer restarts (Windows' `TaskbarCreated`) and sized for the screen's scaling. Off Windows it is a stand-in, so the Pi and the tests are unchanged.
- **What the menu holds is worked out in plain Python** (`voice_assistant/app_menu.py`): the lines from the satellite's state, which icon shows, the hover text. The shortcut's text is read, checked and written in `voice_assistant/shortcut.py`. The Windows module only draws and registers what they say, so the Mac's menu-bar app (piece 4) adds its own drawing and nothing else. Names in settings and messages say "computer" and "shortcut", never "PC" or "Windows".
- **The items.** Listen is the same as a press of the key, so pressed while recording it sends what was said, and over a reply it stops it and listens. Play / Pause and Next are the media key's actions (0031) for this computer's own music, and are greyed while not connected; Play / Pause reads "Pause" while music plays here, "Play" otherwise, and "Play / Pause" if the brain never said. The menu forgets what was playing when the brain is lost, so it never offers "Pause" for music it can no longer know about. Settings opens the settings page at its Satellites section, from the address the page already holds (`wrapped.link`), which the brain sends with the state; there is no second address setting, and the line is greyed when none is known.
- **Mute microphone is done on the computer itself**, so it works with the brain away: the satellite mutes Windows' microphone, starts the time limit (`computer.mic_mute_minutes`, which the brain now sends with the state) and plays `mic_off.wav` or `mic_on.wav` itself. It does what "mute the mic" said at the PC does, and a press still opens the microphone for one turn. The tick and the muted icon mean Windows' microphone, muted by Nova or by hand, so the satellite now looks at it always, often enough that a mute by hand shows within a few seconds, and afresh as the menu opens. Choosing the ticked line while a press holds the microphone open turns it on, and it stays on after the turn: he asked for it on, and the end of the turn must not undo that.
- **The icon's states.** Ready, microphone muted (a red slash) and not connected (grey), with hover text "Nova", "Nova: microphone muted" and "Nova: not connected". Not connected wins over muted: a grey icon says Nova can't be reached, which matters more than a mute which still holds. The file for ready is `nova.ico`, the one Start menu, mixer and `Nova.exe` use too.
- **The icon is drawn, not painted.** `scripts/make_icons.py` draws it from the orb's own drawing (`orb_drawing.py`) at 16, 20, 24, 32, 48 and 256 px; the files are committed (`scripts/windows/icons/`), and nothing draws on the PC. Up to 48 px the orb is tight: a solid disc with the white core, so it reads on a light taskbar as well as a dark one. Only the 256 px size keeps the full glow. `preview.png` beside them shows every state at every size on a dark and a light strip.
- **The shortcut** is registered with Windows itself (`RegisterHotKey`) on the tray's thread, and a press of it is the same press as Numpad 3's. One per computer, set on the settings page under Satellites ("Shortcut to listen", beside "Also called", and kept with it in `SatelliteRooms`), written like `ctrl+alt+n`. Empty means none. It must have one of Ctrl, Alt, Shift or Win with one other key (a letter, a digit or the space bar), or be a function key, F1 to F24, alone; anything else is refused on the page with a plain reason. Numpad keys alone aren't allowed: with Num Lock off they are Page Down and the like, which is why `flow100.ahk` keeps Numpad 3. A change reaches the PC at once, as the old one is let go and the new one registered. On a Mac "alt" will mean Option; that mapping is piece 4's.
- **A shortcut another program holds.** Windows refuses it. The satellite tells the brain (`shortcut_taken`), the page says beside the box "In use by another program on that computer.", and the menu's Listen shows no shortcut. The brain keeps it per connection and the satellite tells it again on each connection, so a restarted brain, or a new connection, is never left with a stale answer. Nova goes on working by Numpad 3 and the menu. An old brain which sends no shortcut leaves the default; an old satellite ignores it.
- **`Nova.exe`.** The first task of the build was a throwaway test on his PC (20 November, 23:21), which proved by its log that this works: a `Nova.exe`, compiled on the PC by PowerShell's `Add-Type` with the orb's icon, runs the venv's Python inside itself. Both processes, the launcher and the satellite, show as "Nova" with the description "Nova". Packages import, arguments, stderr and exit codes pass through, and sound and the microphone work from it. A tray icon and Ctrl+Alt+N worked from the windowless process. That was route 1 of the spec, so the fallback (naming the audio session from inside `pythonw`) was not needed. It is built by `install.ps1` from `scripts/windows/nova_exe.cs`, the icon and `nova.manifest`, kept in the venv (`venv\Scripts`, where it also finds `pyvenv.cfg`) so the code's mirror never deletes it, and built again only when one of those changed. It finds the base install from `pyvenv.cfg`, loads its `python3NN.dll` and calls `Py_Main`, which is all `pythonw.exe` does. Its stack size is copied from the base `pythonw.exe`. A failed build is a warning, not a failed install: an earlier `Nova.exe` is used if there is one, else `pythonw.exe`, as before.
- **One process tree, every part of it Nova.** The launcher still starts the satellite and starts it again whenever it exits, one launcher at a time by the same mutex (`Local\NovaSatelliteLauncher`), so a new launcher and an old `pythonw` one still shut each other out. Both are started through `Nova.exe`. The satellite ends with exit code 10 for Quit, which ends the launcher too, and 11 for Restart, which starts it again after the shortest wait. Both end through the satellite's own clean-up, so neither is counted by the next start as a crash.
- **Two scheduled tasks.** `Nova satellite` starts the launcher at logon, with `--logon`. `Nova start` has no trigger and is what a deploy starts. The launcher tells the three starts apart by its argument: `--logon`, `--open` (the Start menu's) and none (a deploy's). A deploy's start is never taken for a logon, so it can't be stopped by Start with Windows being off.
- **Start with Windows is Nova's own mark, not the task's on/off.** The logon task is registered from an administrator's ssh session, which his own session may not be allowed to change, and `install.ps1` registers it afresh on every deploy, which would switch it back on. So turning it off leaves a file, `no_logon_start`, in Nova's data folder; a start with `--logon` ends at once when it is there, before it touches anything; install never touches it; and the task stays for a deploy to use.
- **The Start menu's "Nova" opens the page.** Its shortcut passes `--open`. While Nova runs, the second launcher asks the running satellite (through the press listener's local port, with the same header the press needs) to open the settings page, and ends: no second Nova. After a Quit it starts Nova. The shortcut keeps its AppUserModelID `Nova.Satellite` (0031), and now has the orb's icon, which is also what puts it in the media pop-up.
- **A deploy starts Nova again only if it was running when the deploy began, or on a first install.** If he had quit it, it stays quit, with the new code ready for the next start. With Start with Windows off and Nova running, a deploy still starts it again. The deploy finds the running app by `Nova.exe` from the install's `venv\Scripts`, and still by the old `pythonw` command lines, so the first deploy over an old install stops that.

**What it costs.**

- **More Windows-only code** in the satellite's process: the tray's window, its menu and the shortcut, about 850 lines in `tray.py`, all of it needing his PC to be proven.
- **`Nova.exe` is one more thing to build** on the PC at install, and its first build is in the first deploy. Windows' own compiler makes it, so nothing is installed, but it can fail (then `pythonw.exe` runs Nova and Task Manager says Python again, with a warning in the deploy's output).
- **The main thread's COM changes.** Under `Nova.exe` it is an explicit multithreaded apartment, which the .NET runtime sets before Python starts; under `pythonw` it is uninitialised. Everything in the test worked under it, and the satellite imports comtypes and winrt only on threads of their own. If anything in Windows' volume, microphone or media calls ever misbehaves only under `Nova.exe`, this is the first place to look.
- **Not yet seen by him: what the volume mixer and Windows' microphone list call it, and the icon in Task Manager.** The process's name and description are proven, and the mixer and the microphone notice normally follow them, but only his eyes on his PC show it (checks 87, 88 and 90). If the mixer says anything but Nova, the remedy is planned: name the audio session through Core Audio's `IAudioSessionControl` (its display name and icon path), which `pc_volume.py` already reaches through comtypes.
- **A new tray icon is tucked behind Windows' arrow** until he drags it out once. An app can't reliably force that, so it is a line in the release checklist.
- **A restart leaves no icon for about two seconds**, and a satellite which can't start has none at all.
- **A few full-screen games hide registered shortcuts** from every program. Numpad 3 through AutoHotkey still works there.

**Not done.** The Mac's menu-bar app (piece 4), which takes the shared menu, shortcut and icon states and adds its own drawing; fingerprint approval (piece 3), which this leaves room for, as a named process with a window handle of its own is what a Windows Hello prompt needs to say "Nova"; an installer or an entry in Windows' installed apps; notifications (toasts); a settings window, as every setting stays on the one page.

**Anti-goals** (a failure even if it works). A press, the chime, a reply or music waiting on the tray, the menu or the shortcut, or failing because of them; stealing focus or a keystroke from a game or from typing (the menu takes focus only while it is open); a console window flashing up at logon, on a restart or on a deploy; Quit leaving something behind (after it there is no Nova process, no tray icon, the microphone is released, and nothing starts it again until the next logon or the Start menu); two Novas at once, however started; a deploy which can no longer find and stop the running app; the tray icon staying on "ready" when the microphone is muted or the brain is gone.

**Things to know.**

- The menu closes on a click elsewhere or Escape, as every tray menu does, and a click on the icon while it is up closes it without opening another: Windows passes that same click on to the icon, so a click within 0.3 s of the menu closing is taken as the one which closed it. Whether this feels right is for his hands.
- The icon and its hover text come from the last look at Windows' microphone and never wait on it, so a stuck Windows call can't hold up a press or a click. Only as the menu opens does the tray wait for a fresh look. While a press holds a muted microphone open for its one turn, the icon and the tick still say muted.
- Whether the icon is sharp at his screen's scaling, comes back after Explorer restarts, and is refused cleanly when another program holds the shortcut can only be seen on the PC. A check over ssh runs in another session and can't put anything on his desktop.
- `scripts/windows/check_app.ps1` shows how Nova stands after a deploy (each Nova process, `Nova.exe`, both tasks, the shortcut, the logon mark) and only looks.
- The shortcut's brain side (`shortcut`, the page's address, the mute's minutes) needs the brain restarted before the PC shows them; until then the PC uses the defaults.

**Setting it up (his named jobs, in order).** Push to GitHub main and restart `nova-backend` (the brain sends the shortcut, the page's address and the mute's minutes, and takes `shortcut_taken`); the PC's `scripts/deploy_pc.sh`, then `scripts/windows/check_app.ps1`. The Pi needs nothing. Then the checks in the release checklist (71 to 95), warning before sound.
