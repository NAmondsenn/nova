# 0015: Football, round two: South Shields from BBC Sport, line-ups, and a fortnight on the card

14 November 2026 · Agreed with the user in chat · Built

**Context.** Football is built on both sides as decision 0014 has it, and not yet deployed. Home Assistant's `football.py` reads ESPN's free feed; Nova reads `sensor.football_teams` and asks `shell_command.football_ask`. Three things came up once it was built.

- ESPN has none of South Shields' league matches: it has no National League North, and only lists their FA Cup ties. One of the user's four followed teams is therefore missing most of the season, on the card, in Nova's answers and in its announcements.
- The card on the Home view shows today's and tomorrow's matches and then each other followed team's next fixture, however far off it is. A national side can sit there for months between international breaks.
- Nova has no line-ups: asked for one, it says Home Assistant's football doesn't have it.

What a look on 14 November showed, all read-only:

- BBC Sport's page for a team (`https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/<team>/scores-fixtures`, and the same with `/<YYYY-MM>` for a month) carries the team's matches as data inside the page, with no key. For South Shields it listed their National League North matches (as "National League N / S"), their FA Cup ties and their FA Trophy tie of that day, which it showed live, 0-0 at "46'", and then at full time, 0-1. Each match has BBC's own id, the kick-off, `PreEvent`, `MidEvent` or `PostEvent`, a label (`"46'"`, `"FT"`), both sides' full and short names and scores, the competition and its stage ("2nd Round"), and the winner. A page is about 350 KB. It is unofficial, as ESPN's feed is, so its shape can change.
- BBC's addresses for teams follow their names: `south-shields`, `stevenage`, `liverpool`, `england`, `manchester-united`, `brighton-and-hove-albion`, `afc-bournemouth`, `nottingham-forest`. An address that is no team's still answers, with no matches.
- The FA Trophy tie of 14 November was on BBC Sport and on the club's own site, and missing from FotMob.
- TheSportsDB's free tier lists South Shields' fixtures and results, but has no live score.
- ESPN's match summary (`.../soccer/all/summary?event=<id>`) has each side's line-up once it is out: the formation, and every player with whether they start. It was seen for a Liverpool match and a Stevenage cup tie, and for matches about to start: one side's line-up can be out before the other's, about an hour before kick-off.

**What the user wants, and why.** South Shields as fully as the other teams, in Home Assistant if at all possible: when they play, in the league and the cups, and the live score and minute. He doesn't need their odds or the like. Searching the web from Nova was his fallback, which he called not ideal, as it adds a dependency to Nova. On the card, a team's next match only when it is within a week or two, so that England show around international breaks and the summer isn't a long list. And line-ups by voice: "what's the starting 11 for Liverpool?", with the bench, for one team or both. Everything else built in round one he was happy with.

**Approaches.**

- *BBC Sport as a second source inside Home Assistant's script.* The same script reads BBC's page for each followed team and adds the matches ESPN doesn't list. The card, Nova's answers and the announcements all get South Shields with nothing new in Nova. Picked.
- *Nova searches the web for South Shields.* A new dependency in Nova, slow, no reliable live minute, and nothing on the card.
- *TheSportsDB.* Fixtures and results, but no live score on the free tier.
- *BBC Sport instead of ESPN.* It would lose what only ESPN gives: finding any team or competition by name, cup rounds, grounds, odds and line-ups.

For the card, the window is a number in Home Assistant's package (see 6), not a helper: it is changed rarely, and a new helper is more than it needs.

**Decision.**

1. **ESPN first; BBC fills in, for followed teams.** For each followed team, Home Assistant's script also reads the team's matches from BBC Sport, and adds a BBC match only where ESPN lists no match for that team kicking off within 12 hours of it. Where both list a match, ESPN's stands. So Liverpool, Stevenage and England gain nothing in practice, and South Shields gain their league and FA Trophy matches while their FA Cup ties still come from ESPN. This holds for the sensor (the card and Nova's announcements) and for a followed team asked about through `football_ask`, which are built from the same lists. A team that isn't followed is asked about from ESPN alone, as in round one: finding a new team on BBC costs up to a few pages of about 350 KB inside Nova's 20 seconds, while a followed team's BBC pages are already in the cache the sensor keeps. To have a non-league side's league matches, follow it.

   Two more rules keep ESPN's matches ESPN's. The script keeps a team's next five fixtures and last five results, which is as many as ESPN's lists for a team go to, so when ESPN lists five, BBC adds no fixture kicking off beyond ESPN's fifth and no result from before its fifth: those are matches ESPN has and isn't listing. And a lone cup tie, which ESPN drops from a team's fixtures at kick-off and gives a result for only hours later, keeps its competition for a day after kick-off, so that ESPN's scoreboard still lists it and its match keeps ESPN's id from kick-off to the whistle, with no BBC copy under another id beside it.

2. **Finding the team on BBC.** The address is made from the team's name as ESPN gives it in full, then its short name, then the box entry: lower case, "&" as "and", anything else not a letter or digit as a hyphen. An address counts only once BBC's matches there have a side of that name; a team with none gets nothing from BBC, and that is remembered for a day. A page that can't be read, or no longer holds BBC's data in the shape the script knows, is a failure (5), never taken for "no such team". There is no list of BBC addresses.
3. **A BBC match is a Contract match.** Its `id` is `bbc:` and BBC's id; the followed team's side carries the team's ESPN id, so that Nova knows which side is followed, and the other side `bbc:` and BBC's id for it. `home` and `away` are BBC's short names and `home_name` and `away_name` its full ones where they differ; scores once it has started; `clock` BBC's label; `state` from BBC's status; `winner` from BBC's. `league` is the competition's name as BBC gives it, and `slug` is `bbc:` and the last part of BBC's address for the competition (`bbc:fa-trophy`). A BBC match has no `venue`, `odds` or line-up, and no crest on either side: ESPN has none for South Shields at its usual address.

   `phase` is set only from what BBC's own data has been seen to say, in live matches captured on 14 November. BBC has four statuses: `PreEvent`, labelled "Scheduled" (state `pre`); `MidEvent`, labelled with the minute ("89'"), the minute with added time ("90'+1") or "HT" (state `in`); `PostEvent`, labelled "FT", or "PENS" for a match settled on penalties, whose score BBC keeps in each side's `runningScores.penaltyShootout` (state `post`); and `Postponed` (state `post`). "HT" is half time. "FT" is full time, and so is "PENS", with the shoot-out. A minute is the first half up to 45 and the second up to 90, and one past 90 is taken for extra time, which none of those matches reached. `Postponed`, or a comment or label saying postponed, abandoned or cancelled, is a match off and over, whatever its status. Any other status is a match left out, and any other label leaves `phase` out, so that Nova says goals but not half time or full time for it.

4. **Live from BBC.** A BBC page is read again on every run (once a minute) while a match on it is in play or about to start (within ten minutes of its kick-off, or listed as still to come up to three hours after it), and otherwise every ten minutes, or every six hours once every match on it is over. A BBC match of today is the team's `now` whatever its state, so that full time is announced, as ESPN's is from the day's scoreboard; one still in play after midnight is too.

   BBC is an extra, so it is read after ESPN's part and what a run reads of it is limited. Each page is read at most once a run, whatever asks for it. A team has at most five pages a run: its own page (today's), this month's fixtures, this month's results, and next month's and last month's. On a day a team is looked for again (2), a few more are read, for the other spellings of its name. A read gives up after BBC's own six seconds, and none starts once 20 seconds of the run have passed. A team's pages are read together, not one after another.

5. **When BBC can't be read, it fails quietly.** A failed BBC read never fails the run, never touches ESPN's part, and never marks a team or the sensor stale: the user wants South Shields' score as a nice extra, not as something Nova goes quiet or apologises about. The last good copy stands in for up to two days for fixtures and results, but a match in play from an old copy is left out, so an old score is never shown or said as current. A match BBC lists as still to come is left out too when its day has gone or its kick-off was more than three hours ago, as one that had started would read in play, so it can only be an old copy's. With no copy (straight after a deploy, which deletes the cache, or after two days of BBC being down) the team simply has ESPN's matches. The user agreed this on 14 November, in place of marking the team stale.
6. **A fortnight on the card.** The card's rows are today's and tomorrow's matches, then each followed team's next match only if it kicks off within the next 14 days. The card is shown whenever there is a row, not only when someone plays today or tomorrow. The 14 is passed to the script in the package's command (`--card-days=14`), so changing it is a one-line edit and a reload. `sensor.football_soon` counts the card's rows, so that the card shows while there is one. The card's template only lists the rows, and is unchanged. Nothing else in the sensor changes. A BBC match has no crest on either side, so none on the card.
7. **Line-ups.** `football_ask` takes a fifth question, `lineups`, for a team. It answers for one match: the team's match in play; else its next if it kicks off within 24 hours; else its last if that was within the last 24 hours; else its next, whenever that is. The answer has the match and, for each side whose line-up ESPN lists, the formation, the starting eleven and the bench, by name. Before a line-up is out, or for a match ESPN doesn't carry, there are no sides, and Nova says so with the kick-off: line-ups usually come about an hour before. Nova's `get_football` tool gains `about: "lineups"`, for a team; with no team named it is the followed team playing now or today. Claude replies with the side asked for, or both. For a match from BBC, which never has line-ups, or one already over with none, Nova says no line-ups are listed for it rather than when they come.

   A side is named as the match names it: its short name, and its full name where that differs, which is how Nova tells which side of the match a line-up is. The script keeps ESPN's summary of a match as its line-ups only, as the whole runs to about 200 KB. Asked with a team and a competition ("who's starting for Liverpool in the Champions League tonight?"), a team's line-ups are still asked of the team; a competition alone asked for line-ups is asked for its matches.

   Nova's words for them. "Line-ups aren't out yet; they usually come about an hour before kick-off." is said only of a match still to come, and not of one from BBC. A match in play, over, off or from BBC with none out gets "No line-ups are listed for it.". Where only one side's is out, the other's "isn't out yet" for a match still to come and "isn't listed" for any other. With no team named, the team is the followed one in play; else the one with a match still to come today, the earliest first; else the one whose match today is over; and it is asked for by its entry in the teams box, the name Home Assistant is known to find it by. With none playing today, Nova says so and asks for a team. A stale team is passed over, as its match is an old copy; where none passed over has a match today and a stale one may, it is "I can't get football right now."

8. **The overview says the teams it can get.** Asked about the followed teams in general ("is anyone playing today?"), Nova says the teams whose matches it can vouch for and names the others: "I can't get South Shields right now." Round one said all or nothing, so that a line from an old copy couldn't pass for a current one; naming the team keeps that, and one team's data being old no longer silences the others. With every team stale, or a Home Assistant script too old to mark teams, it is "I can't get football right now." as before.
9. **Nova's words name no source.** Where Nova's words say ESPN lists no odds for a match, they say "No odds are listed for it."; where they say ESPN doesn't seem to cover a team's league, they say Home Assistant's football doesn't seem to. "ESPN found nothing called X" stays, as only ESPN searches names. The tool's description tells Claude to say fixtures, scores, results, tables, line-ups, who is left, the ground and the odds only from what Home Assistant gave, never from memory: odds from memory would be a made-up figure, and so would the rest.
10. **Branches and deploy.** Round two is built on new branches from round one's heads: `football-2` in Nova's worktree and `football-ask-2` in Home Assistant's. Either both rounds go live together, or round one first and then round two, each on the user's named job; Home Assistant's side first in both cases. Deleting the script's cache file after the copy is part of every deploy of the script.

**The contract, round two.** Round one's table stands, with these additions, word for word the same in both plans:

- A match from BBC Sport has an `id` and a `slug` beginning `bbc:`. Of its two sides, the followed team's carries the team's ESPN id; the other's begins `bbc:`. It never has `venue` or `odds`.
- `shell_command.football_ask` takes `what` = `lineups`, with `name` the team. The answer: `team` (`id`, `name`, `full_name`?, `followed`), `match`? (the match the line-ups are for, as above), `sides`? (each `team` (short name), `team_name`?, `formation`?, `starters` (names, in ESPN's order), `bench` (names)), with `stale`, `error` and `unknown` as every answer has them. `sides` holds only the sides whose line-up is out.
- The sensor's `rows` are today's and tomorrow's matches, then each team's next match within the card's days.

**Goals.** South Shields' league and cup matches on the card, in Nova's answers and in its announcements, live; England on the card only when they play within the fortnight; a team's line-up by voice.

**Not in this round.** Tables, rounds or "who's left" for a competition only BBC carries (the National League North table, who is left in the FA Trophy); asking about such a competition by name; odds, grounds and line-ups for BBC matches; BBC for anything ESPN already lists; scorers.

**Must not happen.** A followed team going quiet, being called unavailable, or the card emptying, because BBC is down. A match listed twice. A BBC score said as current from an old copy. Nova announcing half time or full time from a BBC label nobody has seen.
