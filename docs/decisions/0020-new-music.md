# 0020: New music, found and fetched

16 November 2026 · Agreed with the user in chat · Built

**Context.** The user wants a much bigger music library, so that his player is more like Spotify and he isn't always hearing the same songs. Storage is about to stop being a limit. Of two options he chose tracks picked to his taste, Spotify-style, over whole albums from similar artists. The design is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-16-new-music-design.md`, the plan in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-16-new-music.md`.

**SoulSync picks and downloads; Nova adds the voice, the following and the playlist he opens.** SoulSync already keeps a pool of tracks by artists similar to his, makes personalised playlists from it and from his listening, wishlists what the library lacks and downloads it, and downloads new releases of the artists on its watchlist. None of the playlists were made and the watchlist held one artist. So the work is to switch SoulSync's own parts on, and to add only what it lacks. Two other approaches were rejected:

- *Nova schedules everything, from its settings page.* A second scheduler and a second history beside SoulSync's, more code, and more to break on a SoulSync update.
- *Lidarr with import lists.* It adds whole albums and artists, which is the option he turned down.

**The playlists, by a one-off script.** `services/soulsync/discovery-setup.py` in the private homelab repository sets the sizes of SoulSync's playlists (Discovery Shuffle, Hidden Gems, Your Listening Mix, and two Daily Mixes) and creates two of SoulSync's `personalized_pipeline` automations, one weekly and one nightly, which refresh the playlists, sync them to Navidrome and wishlist what is missing; SoulSync's existing automations then download the wishlist. It is idempotent, a dry run unless given `--apply`, and run once on his named job. Day-to-day tuning stays in SoulSync's own pages: Nova holds none of it. The sizes are mine to start from and his to change.

The discovery playlists are where the new songs come from, not something he opens. Discovery Shuffle and Hidden Gems only ever pick tracks he doesn't own, and SoulSync makes no Navidrome playlist of a personalised one which matches nothing there; its re-sync after downloads is for mirrored playlists only. So they never reach Navidrome, while their songs still download. A playlist which matches songs he owns, such as a Daily Mix, does reach Navidrome as SoulSync's own.

**Nova's New Music playlist.** What he opens in his player, and what "find me something new" plays, is a playlist Nova keeps in Navidrome itself (`new_music.py`), decided on 16 November after the final review found that the discovery playlists never arrive. Once a day, at its first look from 6 in the morning (SoulSync's nightly run is at 4), Nova gathers the songs which came into Navidrome in the last `new_music.days` days (14 by default, 1 to 60), newest first and at most 500, and makes or replaces the playlist `new_music.playlist` names, "New Music" by default. Nova only ever writes the playlist it made itself: it notes the playlist's id (and the name it was made under) in its small record when it makes it, and replaces the songs of that playlist by its id, set public so his own Navidrome user sees it. It never picks a playlist to write by name, because Nova signs in to Navidrome as the same user who owns all of his playlists, so a playlist called "New Music" which he made himself would otherwise be emptied each morning. If he deletes Nova's, or there is no id noted yet, Nova makes a new one under the setting's name and notes its id; a new name in the settings does the same and leaves the old playlist for him to delete. If Navidrome doesn't say which playlist it made, Nova takes the one of that exact name which wasn't there before, and if it can't tell, it makes no second one: the look fails and is tried again at the next hourly one. A name with no letters or digits (only punctuation, emoji or other scripts) is refused on the settings page, and skipped with a log line if it gets in anyway. Nothing new leaves it as it is; Navidrome down is tried again at the next hourly look that day. No other playlist is touched, and only counts are logged. It holds whatever arrived, his own downloads and the clean-up's replacements as well as SoulSync's finds, which is fine.

**Following his top artists, in Nova.** `following.py` has a watcher which, once a month from 3 in the morning, puts his top artists of the last 12 months, as the listening stats already give them, on SoulSync's watchlist; SoulSync's daily watchlist scan then downloads their new releases. The stats' artist ids are SoulSync's library ids, which the watchlist doesn't take as they are, so each artist is added by the provider ids the library holds for it, with an explicit source and the other ids linked. What makes the right artist followed is that those ids are SoulSync's own enrichment ids for that library artist, the same trust as SoulSync's own watch button. Nova also reads back each new row's name and takes off one which differs, but SoulSync stores the name Nova sends, so that check can't catch a wrong id; it stays only as harmless defence. It only ever adds: an artist he stops playing stays followed, and one already watched is left alone. A new follow brings the last month of releases, then each new one, never the back catalogue. His listening is never logged, only counts. How many to follow is `new_music.follow_top_artists`, 25 by default and 0 to switch it off.

**By voice.** "Find me something new" plays `new_music.playlist`, Nova's New Music playlist above, which `play_playlist` plays by the id Nova noted when it made it, so none of his own of the same name is taken for it; before Nova has made one under the current name it asks for an exact name, never a near miss. It is Navidrome only, and if the playlist isn't made yet Nova says so. "What's new this week?" reads from Navidrome's newest albums, whatever brought them in, how many albums and songs and the artists with the most, for up to 31 days back; it reads them 200 at a time, up to 4,000, and says "at least" past that. The clean-up's replacements count as new, which is fine. "Play my Daily Mix" already worked.

**Explicit, never clean.** SoulSync downloads explicit versions, as he wants. Nothing here changes that setting.

**Consequences.**

- Nova leans on SoulSync's own API for the watchlist and the library artist, which is not its versioned one, and its image is `:latest`. A change shows only in Nova's log, never aloud: a watchlist it can't read leaves the month to the next look, while a changed add or library route counts that month's artists as refused or not found, and the month as done.
- Nova makes and replaces one playlist in Navidrome, its own. Renaming it on the settings page makes a new one the next morning and leaves the old one for him to delete.
- Soulseek's speed, and the 500 GB music disk, are the limits. A rough 150 to 300 new songs a week is an estimate, not a cap, and the Daily Mixes, being mostly songs he owns, add few. The 12 TB disk at Christmas removes the disk limit.
- Not built: removing what he skips, whole discographies of similar artists, recommendations from a language model (waits for the AI machine), and Fresh Tape and Archives, which need a linked Spotify account.
