# 0036: Approval for big actions

20 November 2026 · Asked for by the user in chat (his call of 16 Nov: "real proof, works away from home, costs nothing"; the PC's Windows Hello added on 20 Nov) · Design: [the spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-approval-for-big-actions-design.md) · Built; his named deploys and hand checks (71 to 80) to come

**Context.** Nova can now do things that cost money, run code or delete his data. A misheard request, someone else in the room, a TV or a prompt slipped into something Claude read must not be able to do those things on their own. He wanted real proof that it is him, from something only he holds: a passkey on his phone, and Windows Hello at the PC (his hand is bandaged, so he'll enrol a finger of the other hand; both fall back to a PIN).

**What he decided.**

1. **What is big.** Large downloads (three or more albums); anything that runs Claude Code or the lab agent; deleting calendar events or playlists; and, in future, anything that reaches outside his room (unlocking doors, sending messages). Only the calendar removal and the album rule exist today. The mark is one call (`Approvals.gate`) for a new feature.
2. **Not big: shutting down or restarting the PC.** They also stop asking "Shut down the PC?" and act at once, confirmed by the chime like every other PC power command (the separate small change in the same deploy; 0034 and 0008 now say so).
3. **Approach 1: a passkey (WebAuthn) on Nova's own page for the phone, Windows Hello for the PC.** The others, and why not:
   - *Home Assistant's "unlock to tap" notification:* least work and HA-first, but the proof is only an HA event which anything with HA admin could fake, and the unlock isn't bound to the request.
   - *A passkey everywhere, the PC included:* one mechanism, but a browser window at the PC is clunkier than Hello's own prompt.
4. **Asked everywhere at once.** The phone always gets a notification; the PC, if it is connected and may approve, shows a Hello prompt at the same moment. The first answer counts and the other is withdrawn (the PC's prompt cancelled; the phone's page says "Already answered."). The Mac's Touch ID joins the same way when the Mac satellite exists.
5. **One approval lasts five minutes** and covers any big action asked in that time, on any device.
6. **The words, exactly.** "That needs your OK. Check your phone." (or "Check your phone or the PC." when the PC can answer, or "Check the PC." when only it can); "Okay, I won't." after a refusal; "No answer, so I haven't." on a timeout; "That needs your OK, but there's no way to give it yet. Add a passkey on Nova's settings page." when nothing can approve; the notification's title "Approve?"; the phone page's "Already answered.".

**What was built.**

- **The service** (`voice_assistant/approvals.py`). `Approvals.gate(description, do, asked_at, done)` is called by a big action after it has worked out what it will do, so the prompt names the real event or album; "no match", "which one?" and "you've already got it" are answered before anything is asked. It says "That needs your OK ..." at once and waits in the background, so the conversation is never held for the wait; the outcome is announced by the existing late-answer path. Each request has its own 32-byte challenge, its description and an expiry, and lives in memory only, so a brain restart refuses everything waiting and closes the window. A brain stop says nothing to whoever was waiting: the request is settled "stopped", nothing runs, and no "No answer" is said, as the brain is going. The first verified answer settles it, opens the trust window if it was an approval and withdraws the others, on every settle including a timeout; a PC whose own wait runs out first takes its prompt down itself. A late answer ends as "Already answered." and settles nothing; the same assertion posted twice settles nothing more; an assertion for another request's challenge never verifies. The last 20 outcomes (time, description, who answered, outcome) are in `approvals.json` in the data folder, never the repo.
- **Several at once.** Two removals in one turn are two requests with their own challenges and descriptions. The PC shows one Hello prompt at a time; the phone gets one notification each. One approval approves the others still waiting through the trust window, logged per request ("by trust"); a refusal settles only its own.
- **The phone and its page.** A high-priority ntfy notification, "Approve?", whose click opens `/approve/<id>` on the settings page's Tailscale address. The page shows the description, Approve and Refuse. Approve runs `navigator.credentials.get` with the request's challenge and user verification required; the brain checks the challenge, type, origin, rp id hash, the user-present and user-verified flags, the signature and the counter (greater than stored, or both 0). Refusing needs no passkey. The page waits as long as Nova does.
- **Passkeys** (`passkeys.json`, up to three: public key, credential id, counter, name, origin; never a private key). **The key is taken as SPKI** (the browser's `getPublicKey()` and `getPublicKeyAlgorithm()`, ES256 and RS256) rather than parsing the COSE structure in the attestation: `cryptography` loads an SPKI directly, and there is far less of our own parsing to get wrong. **`cryptography` is pinned in `requirements-backend.txt`** (50.0.2) and is the one new dependency on the brain.
- **The PC** (`voice_assistant/windows_hello.py`). The hello gains `approves` when Windows Hello is available. `approval {id, description}` shows Hello's prompt with the description as its message, on a thread of its own, and answers `approval_answer {id, approved}` (verified is a yes; cancelled, busy or retries exhausted are a no, and a result never written, or one Windows has no name for, is a no: the raw result starts at -1, never 0, which is VERIFIED). `approval_withdraw {id}` cancels it; an approval which arrives after its withdraw is never shown. The brain accepts an answer only for an id it sent to that satellite, once. A PC which goes away mid-prompt leaves the phone able to answer, and the request still times out on time. **What the Task 0 test found: nothing yet.** The throwaway test of Hello from the windowless satellite hasn't been run (it needs his named job), so the code tries a window of its own through `IUserConsentVerifierInterop` and then falls back to the plain call, and whether the prompt comes to the front, even over a full-screen game, is the first hand check. If neither works, "Can approve" is switched off for the PC and the phone approves alone. It needs `winrt-Windows.Security.Credentials.UI`, added to `requirements-pc.txt`.
- **The marks.** `remove_from_calendar` (always, naming the event and time, "and every time after it" for a series; several at once name each name among them, or what was searched for past three, and "and every time after them" when any is a series) and downloads (the third album within the trust minutes, never fewer than five). Every path goes through the one hook: local intents, Claude, routines, the settings page. With no approval service a big action is refused, never run. Undoing Nova's own recent change (including "undo that" after a removal) is never big.
- **Settings** (section "Approvals", used straight away): `trust_minutes` 5 ("Minutes one approval lasts", 0 to 60), `wait_minutes` 2 ("Minutes to wait for an answer", 1 to 10), `album_count` 3 ("Albums that make a download need approval", 2 to 20), a "Can approve" switch per satellite that says it approves, the passkeys and the recent approvals. Raising any of them needs a fresh approval when a way to approve is set up (see the Final rulings); lowering is free.
- **Tailscale only.** The Approvals section, `/approve/<id>` and `/approve.js`, every `/api/approvals/...` and `/api/passkeys/...` path, and `approvals.*`, `ntfy.phone` and `ntfy.url` in `/api/settings` are refused (403) on the NPM route, whatever its login, and those settings aren't shown there. The relying party is the Tailscale HTTPS address; WebAuthn needs a secure origin.
- **The power commands.** Every PC power command confirms a success with the chime instead of words; sleep, screen off and lock play it and then press the button, as 0034's order was. A failure still speaks. A press which fails after the chime is only logged, not spoken: the chime has already said yes, and a second voice line would contradict it. Opening, closing and searching keep their short spoken reply, since it names what was matched.
- **Find my phone through Google Find My** (the same deploy; spoken in his room, so a hand check). "Find my phone" presses the googlefindmy integration's Play sound for the phone the app's device is, and falls back to the app's notification when Find My hasn't got it or the press fails. Other Find My devices (a tracker tag) are found, rung and placed by their own names, read from Home Assistant at run time. "Stop ringing my phone" and "found it" stop it; a bare "stop" is left to the music and alarms.

**Rulings during the build** (the cost of each is the price of being wrong).

- *A satellite's `can_approve` is the switch; `approves` is the capability.* Both are checked where they meet, in the brain's approvers list, and the page shows the switch only for satellites which said `approves`. The switch is stored, never worked out from the machine (see the Final rulings). Cost: a caller which forgot to check both would ask a machine which can't answer.
- *The trust window never covers removing a passkey.* The spec's "removing a passkey needs another passkey or the PC" wins over blanket trust. Cost: removing a passkey always prompts, even within the five minutes.
- *Security changes need a fresh approval, which the trust window never covers:* adding a passkey, letting a satellite approve and raising a gated setting. The window is for big actions, not for loosening the protection itself. Cost: one more prompt when he changes those settings.
- *Satellite passwords and keys are Tailscale only.* `/api/satellites/password` and `/api/satellites/add`, and anything else which creates or re-keys a satellite, are refused on NPM, or an NPM password could make a satellite which says it is the PC and approves. Cost: satellites are managed only over Tailscale.
- *Adding any passkey, the first included, needs a fresh approval whenever any way to approve is set up* (say the PC is set up first, asleep or not). The spec's "when a way to approve exists", read as set up (see the Final rulings). A passkey begun free isn't added if a passkey or an approving PC was set up meanwhile. Cost: the first passkey then needs the PC's Hello.
- *If the Hello test hasn't run when the PC code is built, follow the plan's fallback* (owner-window interop, else the plain call) and keep the live check on the checklist. Cost: a fix after the live check.
- *Find My counts as down only when its button is "unavailable";* "unknown" is a never-pressed button. Cost: if the integration reports "unknown" while Google is down, Nova may say it is ringing when it is not.
- *Shut down and restart acting at once* (his word, 20 Nov), where 0034 and 0008 had them ask first.

**Final rulings** (from the whole-branch review, 21 Nov; the cost of each is the price of being wrong).

- *"A way to approve exists" means set up, not reachable now.* `Approvals.guarded()` is any passkey stored, or any satellite which may connect that said it approves and is let approve, connected or not; a fault reading the passkeys or telling whether a satellite may approve counts as set up. A satellites' rooms file which can't be read at start does not: it is read as empty, so no satellite counts until its hello stores its switch afresh. Every security change (adding a passkey, the first included; removing one; switching "Can approve" on; raising a gated setting) asks a fresh approval whenever it is true, and when it is true but nothing can be asked now (the phone's topic blank, the PC asleep) is refused with "Nova can't reach your phone or the PC to ask: set the phone's ntfy topic or wake the PC first." `ways()` only chooses whom to ask. The last passkey is still free to remove when no other way is set up. Before this, blanking the phone's topic with the PC asleep made every one of them free. Cost: with the topic blank and the PC away he can't change those until one is back; a broken passkeys file blocks them until it is mended by hand.
- *Where the phone's approvals go is guarded too.* Changing `ntfy.phone` or `ntfy.url` needs a fresh approval when a way is set up, and both are refused through NPM. When nothing can be asked now they are free (my call within the ruling): setting the topic is the way back the refusal names, and a topic alone can't approve anything without his passkey. Cost: one more prompt when he changes them; anyone on the tailnet page could point a blank topic elsewhere, and so read the descriptions of later requests, but not approve them.
- *Albums are counted over the trust minutes, but never fewer than the default five.* With the trust minutes at 0 every album was the first, so no download was ever large. Cost: with a shorter trust window, albums are still counted over five minutes.
- *"Can approve" is stored the first time a PC or Mac says it approves:* on if no way to approve is set up at that moment (first set-up), else off until he switches it on with a fresh approval. A satellite whose stored machine changes to `pc` or `mac` is stored the same way afresh, and one never stored is a no. Otherwise anything with a satellite's password could say it is the PC and be asked, and answer, for itself. Cost: a PC set up after the phone's passkey needs one approval to be let approve; an entry kept from before this, with no switch stored, is a no until its next hello stores one.
- *A new password, or a satellite added or removed, resets its "Can approve".* Renewing its password or adding it stores the switch afresh by the first set-up rule at that moment (on only if no way to approve is set up, else off), and removing it forgets the switch and `approves` entirely, in `SatelliteRooms.forget_approval`, called by the brain's own add, renew and removal, so every caller gets it. Before this, re-keying "pc" or removing and adding it again kept its switch on, so anyone on the tailnet page could mint a password, connect a fake saying it was the PC and approves, and be asked, and answer, for itself. Cost: after a re-key he switches Can approve on again, with an approval.

**My calls** (his to overturn).

- One trust window across all devices, starting from the approval.
- "Three or more albums" means the third album asked for within the trust minutes, or five if they are fewer (one request is at most one album today); single songs never count; "everything by an artist", if it is ever allowed, is big.
- Taking a song off a playlist isn't big; deleting a whole playlist will be, once it exists.
- Undoing a calendar removal isn't big. Refusing needs no passkey. After a refusal or timeout nothing is retried. The phone's notification can't be unsent, so its page says "Already answered.". Waiting requests are refused on a brain restart. Removing a passkey needs an approval when another way is set up; the last can go from the page alone only when no other way is set up.
- Opening, closing and searching keep their spoken reply because it names what was matched.
- Measuring a Find My "ring" by the integration's buttons and a ten-minute memory of what was rung.

**Not done.**

- An approval API for outside callers (the lab agent, Claude Code launched elsewhere): the service is shaped so a later caller is one call, but nothing runs Claude Code from Nova yet.
- Doors and messages, and deleting a whole playlist: none exist. They get the mark when built.
- The Mac's Touch ID, which joins when the Mac satellite does.
- Approving from the Pi or the clock display (no fingerprint reader), proof of presence in the room, voice ID, Home Assistant's notification actions.

**Anti-goals** (a failure even if it works). A big action going ahead without a fresh verified approval or outside an open trust window, including through a crash, a restart, a timeout or a reply from a device which wasn't asked; an approval for one action used for another (replay); Claude being able to skip, fake or pre-approve (the check is in the executor, after Claude has chosen); the approval page usable by anyone but him; a secret or private key anywhere but his phone, or a challenge, assertion, credential id or description-bearing id path in the log.

**Setting it up (his named jobs, in order).** Push to GitHub main. On the brain, **install `cryptography` into the brain's venv as the `nova` user** (`requirements-backend.txt` pins it) and restart `nova-backend`. On the PC, `scripts/deploy_pc.sh` with `requirements-pc.txt` (the Windows Hello package). The Tailscale HTTPS address must already serve the settings page. Then the hand checks in the release checklist (71 to 80), **registering the phone's passkey first**, and warning before sound.
