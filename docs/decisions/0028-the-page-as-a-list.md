# 0028: Nova's page as a list, one section at a time

18 November 2026 · Agreed with the user in chat ("yes b") · Built · Design: [the spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-18-settings-page-list-design.md)

**Context.** The user's words about Nova's settings page, which [0009](0009-the-settings-page.md) made: "I no longer like this page, and I kind of hate it to be honest. It's impossible to navigate. It looks nice, but it's unorganized, cluttered, and yeah, like I said, absolutely impossible to navigate, especially on a phone. It's unusable." He couldn't find the satellites on it. Shown three tappable mock-ups (tabs, a list, a jump bar) he chose the list: "yes b".

The page was thirteen sections stacked in one scroll, with no menu, no search and no way to jump, and every setting Nova lets him change in one form far from the feature it belongs to. He likes how it looks (the night sky, the frosted glass, the type, Nova's star), so that stays. Nothing the page does was to be lost: no control or setting made unreachable, no unsaved change lost by opening another section, going back or the 20-second refresh, and no change to what a control does or to what Nova's API takes or gives, but for one addition (`journeys` in `/api/state`, the asks made to Google this month and the cap, for Journeys) and two new labels (the night's two settings, "Night starts at" and "Night ends at", are now "Quiet hours start at" and "Quiet hours end at").

**Decision.** The page is a list of Nova's sections, and one section opens at a time, in `voice_assistant/web/settings.html`, `settings.js` and a new `settings_list.js`, with no framework, no build step and no new dependency.

1. **A list in four groups.** Under Nova's masthead: what needs attention, a search box, then a row for each of seventeen sections, each with its icon, its name and a line on how it is now. Now: Status, Music, Timers and alarms. Home: Devices, Batteries, Clock displays. Daily: Journeys, Football, Calendars and lists, Routines. Nova: Satellites, Sound and voice, Claude, Notifications, Memory, Keys, Everything else. Five of these are new sections (Journeys, Satellites, Claude, Notifications, Everything else) and one is renamed (Sound became Sound and voice). The list is drawn once; later loads change only a row's text and its warning colour.
2. **One section at a time, and Back.** A tap on a row opens that section alone, from the top, with "All of Nova" at its head. The phone's back gesture and the browser's Back return to the list, scrolled to where it was, and a second Back leaves the page as it would have. The sections stay in the page and are hidden, not removed, so each keeps its state, its unsaved fields and its refresh, which never closes the open section or takes the focus. A section has an address (`#music`), so a link or a reload opens it. The browser's own `popstate` is what shows the list, so the page, the gesture and the button agree. The open section's row is marked as the current one (`aria-current`), and Status's on a wide screen while none is open. While the page is locked behind its login nothing opens, and the address is looked at again once it's let in.
3. **A feature's settings sit with the feature.** One table, `SECTIONS` in `settings_list.js`, says which section holds a setting by the start of its key, and replaces `GROUPS`: Music holds `music.`, `navidrome.`, `soulsync.`, `spotify.`, `clean_check.`, `new_music.` and `wrapped.`; Football `football.`; Journeys `journeys.`; Sound and voice `voice.`, `sounds.` and `thresholds.`; Claude `web_search.`, `conversation.` and `usage.`; Notifications `ntfy.`, with the quiet hours; Clock displays `display.`; and Everything else `home_assistant.`, `mqtt.`, `logging.` and any key no section claims, as such a key lay in Other before. Where a section holds more than one of the old groups they stay as sub-headings (Music and Wrapped; Voice, Sounds and Listening; Home, Privacy and Other). Each setting's input is part of the one settings form by its `form` attribute, wherever it is drawn, so there is still one save bar, as before. It is fixed to the bottom of the screen while anything is changed and unsaved, says how many, saves them all, and keeps the changes as he moves between sections; on a wide screen it is the open section's width, under it. The save bar stays up after a save which needs a restart, offering "Restart now"; a save which hides it puts the focus on the open section's heading, or the search box on the list. While anything is unsaved, leaving the page (a reload, a link, a Back out of it) has the browser check first; the rest of the time it doesn't, as a page which asks is never kept in the browser's back/forward cache. A setting no longer says "Used straight away" under it: only one which waits for a restart says so.
4. **Satellites is its own section.** The satellites with their buttons and the "Travels with me" switch, adding one, the new-password panels, the choice of which satellite the page looks after (its chips captioned "This page looks after"), and the microphone's mute, which had sat in Status. Status keeps its tiles, Recent problems and the restart notice.
5. **The line on each row** says how the thing is, from what the page has already loaded, and is drawn again as that changes. The rules are plain functions in `settings_list.js` over a view which `settings.js` builds (`currentView`): "All well" or the first thing which isn't; the song and artist playing or "Nothing playing"; "1 reminder, 1 alarm" or "Nothing set"; "2 lights on" or "All off"; the lowest battery ("Tablet at 14%", in the warning colour when under its mark); "1 clock display" (the page is told nothing of whether a clock is connected); "21 of 3,000 asks used this month"; "2 teams followed"; "Signed in to Google" or "Sign in to Google"; "2 routines"; "2 of 2 connected"; "Voice at 60%"; "Web search on"; "Quiet from 23:00 to 07:00" or "No quiet hours"; "12 things remembered"; "6 of 11 set"; and nothing for Everything else. A line whose data hasn't loaded, or couldn't, is left empty, never guessed.
6. **What needs attention** is listed above the list, a row each, tapping through to its section, in this order: a restart is needed, Nova's settings file can't be read (in the page's own words, "Nova can't read its saved settings", as what Nova says of the file is a parser's message or a path, which the top of each section's settings says in full), the default satellite isn't connected, a battery is under its mark, Google or Spotify needs signing in to, a problem in the last hour. With none, nothing is shown there. The restart notice itself stays in Status; the list shows it as a row.
7. **Search.** Typing narrows the list to the sections which have every word typed, in their name, their line, their settings' labels or their own words on the page (headings, labels, buttons, placeholders: "mute", "password", "travels"), and under each lists what matched, up to six; a tap on one opens its section with the field, row or panel it is in scrolled to and marked for two seconds, and the focus on it. Nothing is listed by hand: the page's own words are read from the page as it stands, so a control added later is found; a button with no words of its own (Previous song, a remove button) is found by its accessible name, and a setting by the words of its key as well as its label ("ntfy quiet from"), though only the label is shown. Words are matched as plain text, never as a pattern. With nothing matching it says "Nothing on Nova's page matches that." How many sections were found is said to a screen reader, and Enter lets go of the box, so a phone's keyboard closes over the results.
8. **A wide screen** (1,180 px and over, the page's existing wide breakpoint) shows the list down the left and the open section beside it, Status when none is chosen. Between a phone and that, the page is as on a phone.
9. **What was kept.** The sky, the glass, the type, the star and every existing control; the new pieces (rows, the back button, the search box, the attention rows) are made of the same classes, colour tokens and stroked 24 by 24 icons. The login panel, the page's password and the locked state are as they were: locked, nothing but the log-in shows.

**Approaches.**

1. **Keep every section and its drawing code, and add the list and the showing of one section on top (chosen).** Least to get wrong in 3,000 lines which mostly have no tests, and each section keeps its state.
2. Draw each section afresh when it is opened. Cleaner in the end, but it rewrites the page and loses the half-typed and the unsaved unless each section learns to keep them.
3. A page per section from the server. Every tap is a load, the star and the music lose their place, and the server grows routes for what is only layout.

**My calls, not the user's.** He chose a list and "yes b", and wanted nothing lost; these are the designer's and the builder's, his to change.

- Which section each row is in, the groups, and the names of the new sections.
- The wide screen shows the list beside the open section, Status when none is chosen.
- Keys: several are optional, so an unset one is not a fault. The row says how many are set of how many there are, in the ordinary colour, and no attention row is made for a key; a missing key which matters already shows in Status (Claude can't be reached, Home Assistant not connected).
- Satellites: only the default satellite being away needs attention, as that is where warnings and alerts are said; a push-to-talk one is off as a matter of course. The row's line counts them all plainly.
- Search covers every control, not only the settings.
- Status's line says "All well" only when the state was read, so both its tiles and its problems are known, every tile is good and no problem (not a warning) was logged in the last hour. Otherwise it is the first tile which isn't good, in its own words, else "A problem at 14:08" with the latest one's time. No counts, as the page is given only the latest eight. The attention row for a problem follows the same rule.
- Nova's settings file not being readable is an attention row and is said at the top of every section which has settings.
- The browser's history holds exactly one entry of the page's own above the list, however many sections are opened one after another: a row tapped while a section is open (the wide screen's case) replaces that entry, so one Back goes to the list and a second leaves the page.
- Arriving by a link to a section, the page puts the list under it, so Back goes to the list rather than out of the page. Each load of the page marks the history entries it makes with a token of its own, and a reload inside a section is taken as a link too: the entry under it is the list of the load before, and going back to another load's entry is a whole new load, not a `popstate`, which would lose everything unsaved. "All of Nova" goes back only to an entry of this load's, and otherwise makes the address the bare page and shows the list.
- A section's heading takes the focus when it opens, and its row when it closes (the search box, when a search's results are showing without it), so a keyboard or a screen reader is where the eye is.
- The rules of the list (the sections' table, the lines, attention, search, the address's section) live in `settings_list.js`, with no page, given plain data and returning plain data, so Node tests them without a DOM. `settings.js` builds the view and draws what they say. The two scripts are loaded as files of the page's own, which its Content-Security-Policy lets run.
- A line is left empty when its data hasn't loaded or couldn't: each part of the view is null unless the load it comes from last worked, so no row shows an old value as now.
- Something playing with no title says the artist, or "Playing".
- The restart notice lives in Status, and shows on the list as an attention row.
- The page was drawn during the build by a harness on the Pi's browser with stood-in answers, which is not in the repository: CT 109 has no browser, so the tests are Node's over a small stand-in for the DOM, and what the page looks like was only seen there. Before it goes live he sees it: the real files with stood-in answers, as a private Claude artifact for him to try on his phone.
- The sentences which send him to "Settings, under Music" are the server's, and Nova speaks some of them, so they stay as they are. No section is called Settings now, and Music holds its own settings, so they still point at the right place. The page-only sentence for Home Assistant not being connected now says to add its address under Everything else.

**Known limits.**

- Arriving by a link to a section, Chrome skips the list on the browser's Back until the page has been tapped: its own rule for history entries made without a tap. "All of Nova" always works.

**Hardware.** None shapes this.
