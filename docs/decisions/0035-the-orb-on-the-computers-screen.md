# 0035: The orb on the computer's screen

20 November 2026 · Asked for by the user in chat, the plan approved in conversation ("that plan is exactly as I'd have imagined it") · Design: [the spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-pc-screen-indicator-design.md) · Built; his named deploys and hand checks (63 to 70) to come

**Context.** The PC is a push-to-talk satellite (0026): Numpad 3 starts a recording, and the only sign of what Nova was doing was sound - the press chime, then the reply. There was nothing to see whether it was still listening, had stopped and was working on it, or was about to speak. The smart clock beside the Pi answers that with its orb (0013). He wanted the same on the PC: a small orb on the screen while Nova listens, thinks and speaks there, and nothing at all otherwise.

**What he decided.**

1. **The orb is drawn by Nova itself in a window of its own** (approach 1). A see-through, click-through, always-on-top window made through Windows' own calls from Python (ctypes), with each frame drawn in numpy, which the satellite already has, and handed to Windows with its own transparency (`UpdateLayeredWindow`). Nothing new to install on the PC or the Pi. The others, and why not:
   - *The clock's own page in a small browser window* (pywebview over Edge WebView2): the exact CSS orb, but two new packages, a server or file for the page, and WebView2's transparent and click-through windows are unreliable.
   - *Qt (PySide6)*: good transparency and animation, but well over 100 MB to install for one small orb, and a second GUI toolkit in the satellite.
2. **Bottom centre of the main screen, just above the taskbar, about twice the size of a taskbar icon.** The size is the bright body, the lobes and core, which is what reads as "the orb": 64 px at 100% scaling. The aura's glow fades out around it to about 1.7 times that width, as on the clock, so the window is that much bigger and its glow, not its body, sits just above the taskbar. (Measuring the size by the body is my reading of "about twice a taskbar icon", his to overturn.)
3. **Shown in screenshots, recordings and screen shares** like any other window: no `SetWindowDisplayAffinity`.
4. **Shown over games**, anti-cheat ones included (Fortnite), with no list of programs it hides for. It is an ordinary window of its own which never touches the game, as Windows' own volume pop-up and Discord's newer overlay are. The on/off setting turns it off if a game ever minds.

**What was built.**

- **What it shows, from what the satellite is doing.** The brain doesn't change how it works. The satellite tells the orb each thing as it happens (`OrbStates`, in `voice_assistant/screen_orb.py`) and the orb works out its own fades. *Listening* from a press starting a recording, or the satellite sending the answer the brain asked to hear (a follow-up), until that recording ends: the brain's `stop_listening`, a second press, or the limit. *Thinking* from the end of the recording until Nova's words start playing, or the brain's turn ends (`allow`) with nothing said; it is dropped after 30 seconds anyway, which no turn takes. *Speaking* while a `play_stream` of kind `reply` or `announcement` plays. *Hidden* otherwise. Listening outranks speaking, so a press over a reply is shown at once, and thinking fills the gap between a recording and the words, so there is no flicker between them. When speaking ends, the orb waits 0.6 s before fading, so a reply in two pieces, or a follow-up listen straight after a question, doesn't make it blink. The fade takes 0.4 s and the grow-in 0.7 s, as on the clock.
- **Speaking is only Nova's own words.** Music, alarms and every `play_sound` file (the press chime, an announcement's chime) never show it, as the clock shows "speaking" only for the words too.
- **The recording's end is told to the orb on the link thread**, as the brain stops it (in `_stop_listening`, and in a second press), not when the main loop notices. The brain's "nothing heard" answer, `allow`, follows `stop_listening` within milliseconds and the main loop can be a piece of audio behind, so told there, `allow` would come first and leave the orb thinking until the 30 seconds were up. A recording dropped or given up on is ended by the main loop's `finally`, which does nothing for one already ended.
- **The window** (`ScreenOrb`, on a thread of its own, as Windows requires). Layered, click-through, topmost, a tool window (no taskbar button, not in Alt-Tab) and no-activate, so it never takes the keyboard from a game or a document. Shown without activating and put back on top each time it appears, as games and other topmost windows can cover it. Placed from the main screen's work area (the screen minus the taskbar), worked out afresh each time it appears, so a resolution change, a moved or hidden taskbar and a different main screen are picked up; sized by the screen's scaling (per-monitor DPI), so it is the same size on a 4K screen at 150% as on 1080p at 100%. About 30 frames a second while it shows. While hidden there is no frame timer and no drawing: the thread only wakes every half-second to answer Windows' messages. Off Windows `ScreenOrb` is a stand-in which ignores everything and imports nothing from Windows, so the Pi and the tests run unchanged. It exists only on a push-to-talk satellite on Windows.
- **The drawing** (`voice_assistant/orb_drawing.py`), plain numpy with no Windows in it. Each frame is premultiplied BGRA: the two auras, the five lobes (electric blue, violet, cyan, indigo, pink-violet) and the white core, each a soft elliptical radial gradient, with the clock's colours, relative sizes, orbits, wobble and per-state speeds. Listening is a slow drift, thinking smaller and quicker, speaking quick ripples with the core pulsing. A frame costs 1.7 ms at 111 px, 3.6 ms at 167 px and 8.9 ms at 250 px on the Pi 5, which the PC beats comfortably.
- **The lobes are laid over each other plainly, and only the core is screen-blended.** In the clock's page every turning orbit is a layer of its own, so a lobe's screen blend never reaches the others; the core, which isn't in an orbit, does blend over the lot. Drawn that way it matches Chromium's render of `display.css` on the Pi to within about 1 of 255 on average (0.55 for thinking, 0.71 for listening, 1.14 for speaking); with the lobes screen-blended too they washed out to a white blob (3.49). The spec first said the lobes were screen-blended; it was corrected to this.
- **Settings**, under Satellites on the settings page, all used straight away: `computer.screen_orb` (on or off, on), `computer.screen_orb_place` (bottom centre, bottom left, bottom right, top centre, top left, top right; bottom centre) and `computer.screen_orb_size` (small, medium, large: 48, 64 and 96 px at 100% scaling; medium). The brain sends them to a push-to-talk satellite in the state it already sends (as `longest_request` is), so a save reaches the PC at once. An old brain which doesn't send them leaves the defaults; an old satellite ignores them; a value the satellite can't make sense of is left alone and logged once.
- **A showing which Windows refuses is dropped and the next one tried.** A locked desktop, a disconnected remote session or the screens changing can make a drawing call fail for a while. That showing is dropped (the window taken down, what it showed forgotten) and the next thing shown starts afresh, with one warning for the first and only debug lines after. Only a window which can't be made at all switches the orb off for the run, with one warning. Ten showings in a row which fail, with no frame going up between, give up until the satellite restarts, as that is a break trying again won't mend; a spoken reminder while the PC is locked is an everyday thing, so it keeps trying through a good few.
- **Nothing waits on the orb.** Every call into it only leaves a note for its thread and returns; any failure in it is logged once and the orb goes quiet. A press, the chime, sending audio and the reply never wait on it or fail because of it.

**Not done.**

- The Mac, which piece 4 builds its own of, on the same settings.
- The words Nova heard under the orb, as the clock shows them. Easy to add later.
- The Pi's conversations on the PC's screen.
- Exclusive full-screen games, which Windows lets nothing draw over. Borderless (windowed full-screen) ones work.
- Pressing the orb to talk: it lets clicks through on purpose.

**Anti-goals** (a failure even if it works). Stealing focus or a keystroke from a game or from typing; a press, the chime or the reply waiting on the orb or failing because of it; visible stutter or a noticeable load on the PC; the orb blinking out between listening and speaking.

**Things to know.**

- The orb shows from when the recording starts being sent, which is after the satellite has sent the wake, unmuted Windows if it was muted and started the press chime, so on a press it can come up a moment after the chime rather than with it.
- A spoken stream which the speaker refuses at once (announcements silenced, a speaker which can't be opened) still counts as speaking while it lasts, and ends thinking.
- Whether Windows draws it over every borderless game is only known by trying: the first game, Fortnite, is his check.
- The look is his to judge. A check over ssh runs in another session and can't put a window on his desktop, so on 20 November the two modules were only run on the PC that way, with the satellite's own Python: the window was made, about 30 frames a second went up through every state and for the top right at the large size, a frame took 2.5 ms to draw and 0.06 ms to hand to Windows, it closed at once, and nothing was logged. What shows over a game, with a moved taskbar or at 150% scaling is for his hand checks.

**Setting it up (his named jobs, in order).** Push to GitHub main and restart `nova-backend` (the brain sends the settings); the PC's `scripts/deploy_pc.sh`. The Pi needs nothing. Then the checks in the release checklist (63 to 70), warning before sound.
