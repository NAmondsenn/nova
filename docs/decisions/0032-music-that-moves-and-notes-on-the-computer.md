# 0032: Music that moves between satellites, and notes on the computer

19 November 2026 · Asked for by the user, built without questions at his word ("just get going") · Design: [the spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-19-music-moves-and-notes-design.md)

Numbered 0032: 0029 (quiet while another speaks), 0030 (changing playlists and the calendar) and 0031 (Windows' media controls) are all published, and this was first numbered 0030 while 0029 was another branch's.

**Context.** The user moves between the Pi and the PC in the bedroom, and the Mac is to come. He asked for the music to follow him ("play it on my Pi or change this to the PC") without starting again, and for something said at a computer to land there as a file he can open ("the PC and Mac satellite, when it's made, can create notes and it saves it to that device's downloads folder"). Both need what 0006 and 0026 did not: Nova has to know which satellite is which machine, in words he'd say. He chose "just get going" over questions, so every call below that is not in his words is mine, listed under "The calls" for him to overturn.

**Decision.** Four pieces.

1. **A satellite is known by what it is, and by what he calls it.** Each satellite's `hello` says what machine it is, `machine` (`pi` for Linux whose device-tree model contains "Raspberry Pi", `pc` for Windows, `mac` for macOS, `linux` for any other), and `saves_notes: true` when it has a Downloads folder (Windows and macOS only). Both are optional: the brain takes a missing or odd value as unknown and never refuses a satellite for it, and an older brain ignores them, so either side can be deployed first. The brain keeps both in `satellite_rooms.json` beside each satellite's kind and area, so they are known while it is away. What names a satellite, after dropping a leading "the" or "my" and a trailing "satellite": its own name (dashes and underscores read as spaces); the names set for it on the settings page; and only if neither fits, its machine's words (`pi`: "pi", "pie", "raspberry pi", "raspberry pie"; `pc`: "pc", "computer", "desktop"; `mac`: "mac", "macbook", "laptop", "computer"). He chose a page name, so it beats a machine word: with "computer" set on the PC, "the computer" means the PC alone. Two satellites matching is said back ("Which one: the PC or the Mac?") and nothing moves. A word matching none is left to Claude, whose tool says "There's no satellite called the phone. There's the Pi and the PC." What a reply calls a satellite is its first page name which isn't one of its own machine's words, else "the Pi", "the PC" or "the Mac", else its own name: "computer" on the PC settles what "the computer" means, and a reply still says "Moved to the PC.", not "Moved to computer.".
2. **Moving music.** "Play it on my Pi", "move this to the PC", "switch the music to the Mac": the music carries on from where it was on the satellite named, with the same queue (in the order before any shuffling), repeat and shuffle, and stops where it was. The source is what is playing, in his room (the satellite he spoke to first) and then anywhere; only when nothing plays, what is paused, in the same order. None gives "There's no music to move." The player's `hand_over` gives up the queue, the song, how far into it (counted from what the satellite's own speaker reports it has played, so a speaker which cannot say counts none of it) and the modes, and empties itself; the target's `take_over` plays from that point. The target keeps its own music volume. Moved music always plays, even from paused. If the target cannot take it, the source takes it back, so it is never left playing nowhere. When Nova is talking in the target's room the target waits for the reply to end, as any music asked for during a reply does; in another room it starts at once. "Play Drake on the PC" starts new music elsewhere, through an optional `on` on `play_music` and `play_playlist`, and is said where he is ("Playing Drake on the PC."), as the PC may be out of earshot. Local rules take the verbs move, switch, change, swap, transfer, take and play with "to", "onto" or "over to", and "on" only after "play", and only when the words name a satellite, so "switch to the next song" is still Claude's and "switch on my PC" is still the PC's power. Claude has a `move_music` tool for the rest. Navidrome only: Spotify has one stream for the whole account, so a move says "Moving music only works with Navidrome."
3. **Notes at the computer.** At a satellite which saves notes, "make a note that the bins go out on Tuesday", "write this down" and "write me notes on the French Revolution" save a plain `.txt` file in that computer's Downloads folder, and Nova says "Saved to your Downloads as Bins." (no extension, which Piper would spell out). Claude's `save_note` tool (`title`, `text`, and `where` for when he names the computer) is offered in every session while any satellite saves notes; the brain saves the note on the computer named, else the one he is at if it saves notes, else the only one that does ("Which one: the PC or the Mac?" when several do, "None of your computers can save notes." when none), and says so when it isn't the one he is at; its description says to use the user's own words when they dictate, written notes of at most about 400 words when they ask for notes on something, and that "add X to my notes" is the list in Home Assistant, not this. The brain sends the satellite `save_note` with an id and the title, the text going as the message's payload rather than in its JSON, which a line of the protocol caps at 4,096 bytes, and waits up to 5 seconds for `note_saved`, which carries the file name used or an error sentence. The satellite writes only into the Downloads folder itself, never a folder inside it, and never over an existing file. The title, which came from speech by way of Claude, is treated as hostile: no path or control characters, no leading dots or spaces, no trailing dots or spaces, Windows' reserved names kept off (a note called "con" becomes "con note"), at most 80 characters, "Note" if nothing is left. Then `.txt`, or " (2)" to " (99)" before it when the name is taken, each file opened exclusively. UTF-8, with Windows line endings on Windows; text over 50,000 characters is refused ("That note's too long to save."). On Windows the Downloads folder is asked of Windows itself, as it can be moved to another drive, falling back to `%USERPROFILE%\Downloads`; on macOS it is `~/Downloads`. Neither the brain nor the satellite logs a note's text as a note, and the log line of what Claude asked for shows it only as its length. The title and file name are logged. What he said is still in the `Processing query` line while transcripts are on, as every utterance is: that setting is his existing control for what he says. Failures are said: "I couldn't save it on the PC: `<why>`." and "The PC didn't answer, so the note wasn't saved."
4. **The page.** Each satellite's row in the settings page's Satellites section says what machine it is ("Raspberry Pi", "PC", "Mac"), whether it "Saves notes to its Downloads", and has an "Also called" box: names apart by commas, at most 5, each at most 30 characters, saved when the box is left (`/api/satellites/called`). A name is refused if it is another satellite's own name or page name, or one of the words for another satellite's machine ("pc" on the Mac while there is a PC), unless it is one of this satellite's own words as well ("computer" on the PC); that stops a nickname from taking another satellite's spoken name away from it. A new satellite is picked up from its `hello`, with nothing to set up.

**The calls.** All made for him, none asked.

- Notes are plain `.txt`, titled by Claude, in Downloads and nowhere else: it is the folder he named, and a file in it is one he knows how to open. No other format, no folder of his choosing, no appending to an earlier note.
- The Pi saves no notes of its own, as it has no Downloads folder he would open, but it can ask for one on a computer: `save_note` is offered in every session while any satellite saves notes, takes an optional `where` as the tools for opening things do, and a note asked for at the Pi lands in the PC's Downloads ("Saved to the Downloads on the PC as Bins."). He asked for a note "on my PC" at the Pi on 20 November, and the first build, which gave the Pi no notes tool, could not do it.
- Moved music always plays, even if it was paused: moving it means wanting it. The target keeps its own music volume.
- "The computer" can mean the PC or the Mac until he names one on the page, and Nova asks which. He can overturn it by giving one of them that word.
- A sleeping PC is not woken to take the music ("The PC isn't connected, so the music's staying on the Pi."): "turn on my PC" is its own command, and a move that wakes a machine slowly is worse than one that says no.
- The replies: "Moved to the PC." and "Saved to your Downloads as Bins."
- The move rule takes "on" only after "play", so "switch on my PC" stays the PC's power (a move there would break a command that exists). If wrong, "move it on the PC" goes to Claude, which has `move_music`.
- "Play music on the PC" with nothing playing anywhere says "There's no music to move." rather than starting music there, which keeps one meaning for the move rule. If wrong, "play something on the PC" is Claude's `play_music` with `on` and no query: it moves the music if there is any, and when there is none anywhere it starts some with the PC's own bare "play" (its liked songs), said as "Playing your liked songs on the PC." The brain's answer to the move says which (`nothing_to_move`), so the words of "There's no music to move." can change without breaking it.
- Moved music takes what is playing before what is merely paused, wherever it is: "it" means the music he hears. If wrong, an old paused queue needs a "play" to come back.
- "Play X on the PC" from a satellite in the same room pauses every other playing player in that room, and keeps it paused when the reply is over, as saying "pause" would: two songs in one room is never wanted. It never pauses Spotify's, which has one stream for the account and so is already gone from the others once it starts on the target. If wrong, "play" at the Pi brings its music back.
- "Play it on my Pi" said at the Pi, with the Pi's own music paused, carries that on; while it plays it says "It's already on the Pi." He means "play it here".
- A satellite with no music player of its own is still offered `move_music`, as it can move another's music; the reply is harmless otherwise.
- A satellite is said by its own name when another one's spoken name is the same, so two Pis are never "the Pi or the Pi".
- The page refuses a name which is another satellite's machine word ("pc" on the Mac while there is a PC), as that satellite could then no longer be found by its own machine word. If wrong, he cannot use a machine word as a nickname on another machine, which would only confuse the matching anyway.
- Whether a satellite saves notes is decided when it starts, from whether it finds its Downloads folder; any failure of that lookup, such as the Windows call, means it does not, and never stops the satellite or its `hello`. A folder made later needs the satellite restarted (Downloads always exists on Windows and macOS). Each save looks for the folder again.
- A dictated note's words stay in the log's `Processing query` line while transcripts are on (see piece 3), and nowhere else.

**Not done here.**

- Waking a sleeping PC to move music to it, and moving Spotify's music.
- Playing on two satellites at once: they would drift apart.
- Notes anywhere but a computer's Downloads folder, so none are saved on the Pi itself (one asked for at the Pi goes to a computer's, as the calls above say); naming a satellite by its room.
- The Mac: the satellite code saves in `~/Downloads` on macOS already, but it has never run on a Mac. It is checked for real when the Mac satellite is built.
- The Windows lookup of the Downloads folder (`SHGetKnownFolderPath` through ctypes) has never run on real Windows: a test replaces it. It falls back to `%USERPROFILE%\Downloads`, and any failure there means no notes rather than a crash, but only the PC can say it works (a live check).
- A long "write me notes on ..." shares Claude's 1,024-token reply limit with its thinking, so a note of about 400 words may not fit. When it does not, the log says Claude stopped short (or gave no reply), nothing is saved, and Nova says "Sorry, that didn't work. Try asking again." Tests cannot say how often that happens (a live check).

**Setting it up (his named jobs).** Merge `music-and-notes` and deploy the brain; update the Pi (so it says it is a Pi) and the PC with `scripts/deploy_pc.sh` (so it says it saves notes). Until a satellite is updated it is still found by its own name or a name on the page, and it saves no notes. Then his checks, in the release checklist's section 35 onwards: music from the Pi to the PC and back mid-song, new music on the other satellite, the PC asleep, "the computer" with page names, a note at the PC, and the page's "Also called" box.
