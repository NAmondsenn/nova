# 0030: Changing playlists and the calendar by voice, and "undo that"

19 November 2026 · Agreed with the user in chat (his three choices below) · Built, not yet live · Design: [the spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-19-playlist-add-and-calendar-remove-design.md)

**Context.** The user's words: "Can you make it so that Nova can add to playlists if I name what playlists to add to? This would be good for when listening to new songs, like the auto-added music. And one more thing is Nova can add calendar events but not remove them." Then: "can it make a new playlist too, make it so i can remove songs off playlists too, like remove this song from this playlist. editing events, i kinda want that too like being able to move one or rename one".

Until now Nova could only read his playlists and add to his calendar: [0008](0008-home-assistant-first.md) says in so many words that adding to the calendar "adds only; never changes or deletes". The music is Navidrome's, and Nova signs in as the user who owns all his playlists there, so it can write to them. But he has six, and a song put on some of them would not stay. Three are remade: New Music by Nova each night, and Daily Mix 1 and 2 by SoulSync's "Nova: daily mixes" automation. The other three are SoulSync mirrors of his old Spotify playlists, and a sync of a mirror makes Navidrome's playlist hold every song in SoulSync's copy: in its default mode ("reconcile") it also removes anything else, and in "append" it never removes but puts back anything missing. A song added to a mirror in reconcile mode would go at the next sync, and a song taken off any mirror would come back. The calendar is Google's own, read and written by Nova directly (`google_calendar.py`), and the sign-in it already has (`calendar.events.owned`) covers changing and deleting events on his own calendars, so nothing needs signing in to again.

**His three choices.**

1. **Removing a calendar event is done straight away, with "undo that"** (option A), not asked first.
2. **SoulSync stops mirroring his three Spotify playlists** (option A). They stay in Navidrome as ordinary playlists. A mirror he makes later is handled by the code: adding to it first switches it to append, removing from it is refused.
3. **"All of them" means from this one on**, as Google Calendar's "this and following events" does. Earlier ones are never changed, so the record of past shifts and lectures stays.

**Decision.** Nova adds the song playing to the playlists he names, takes it off, makes playlists, likes and unlikes it, and removes, moves and renames events on his calendar, one day or from this one on. "Undo that" takes back the last of any of these. The tools are `add_to_playlists`, `take_off_playlists`, `make_playlist`, `like_song`, `make_offered_playlist`, `remove_from_calendar`, `change_calendar_event` and `undo_last_change` in `actions.py`; the playlist rules are in `playlist_changes.py`, the writes to Navidrome in `navidrome.py`, those to SoulSync in `soulsync.py`, those to Google in `google_calendar.py`, and the undo slot in `undo.py`. Nothing is added to the settings page: nothing here is something he would tune.

1. **Which song.** "This" is the song at the queue's place, playing or paused, whoever paused it; "the last song" is the one before it. With two satellites in a room it is the first one playing, as pausing acts on it, else the one paused, as a bare "play" carries on: a song paused on the other satellite is still "this". With nothing queued: "Nothing's playing." The Spotify player says "Changing playlists only works with Navidrome."
2. **The same song** is the one with the same Navidrome id, or the same title and artist as the library helpers compare them, so the album copy and the single, or a clean and an explicit file, count as one; a remix or a live version has another title and is another song. "Already on", "isn't on", removing every copy and undoing all go by this. Taking a song off a playlist removes every copy of it on that playlist.
3. **Adding.** Playlist names are matched loosely, as playing one is, and the reply names the playlists used, so a mishearing shows: "Added Endless to Gym Bangers and Car." A song already there isn't added again: "Endless is already on Gym." Two playlists named and one refused: the other is still done, and the reply says which.
4. **Making a playlist.** "Make a playlist called Chill" makes an empty one; "add this to a new playlist called Chill" makes it with the song. A name already taken (matched exactly, not loosely) isn't made twice: "You've already got Chill." "Add this to Jim" with no playlist near Jim offers: "You haven't got a playlist called Jim. Want me to make it?" A yes makes it with the song that was playing when he was asked, not the one playing by the time he answers: the song travels in the offer. The offer counts as a success, as the download offer does, since a yes is only listened for after one.
5. **Playlists which are remade are left alone, for adding and removing, with the reason.** New Music is known by the id Nova recorded for it; any other is known by SoulSync's own list of what it remakes, asked at the time, so a new mix is covered without Nova being told: "SoulSync remakes Daily Mix 1, so that wouldn't stay." The closest playlist to what he said is the one judged: "my daily mix" is refused for Daily Mix 1, and never falls through to a weaker match which isn't remade.
6. **A playlist SoulSync still mirrors** is matched by `server_playlist_id`, else by `sync_name`, and an empty `sync_name` never matches. Adding first switches it to append, then adds, and says so the first time: "Added Endless to Gym, and set Gym to append only in SoulSync so it stays." If the song is already on it, nothing is switched. If SoulSync's key can't change the mode, the song isn't added and the reply says to set append there. Removing is refused: "SoulSync copies Gym from Spotify, so it would put Endless back. Stop mirroring it in SoulSync and I can." A mirror row which can't be read fails shut: the change isn't made.
7. **SoulSync set up but unreachable:** nothing is added or removed, as Nova can't tell whether it would stay: "I can't reach SoulSync to check Gym, so I haven't changed it." SoulSync not set up: plain Navidrome. Making a new playlist needs no SoulSync check.
8. **Liked songs** are Navidrome's starred songs, as playing already treats "liked songs" (`means_liked_songs`): "like this", "add this to my liked songs" stars the song, "unlike this" unstars it. They are never offered as a playlist to make. "Liked Endless." "You've already liked Endless."
9. **The calendar has one write path.** `events.insert`, which adding already used, now has `events.patch` and `events.delete` beside it, all sent by one function. Every write is sent once, and never again on an unclear answer: Nova says "I can't tell whether Dentist was removed" and sends nothing more. Every write carries `sendUpdates=none`, so nobody is emailed. Home Assistant is not used, as Nova's calendar has never gone through it, and it has no way to write Navidrome playlists either.
10. **Only what is his to change.** An event on a calendar Nova can only read: "I can't change that, Google only lets me change your own calendars." An invite from someone else is left alone, as changing it would tell the organiser: "That's an invite from someone else. Change it in Google Calendar."
11. **Finding the event** is by its words, as `find` does, on the day given or, with no day, from today over the usual 90 days; a time narrows it. Several matches: nothing changes, and Nova lists them (day and time, and the calendar's name when they are on more than one) and asks which; he can name the calendar to narrow them, matched the way adding picks a calendar by name, by one shared helper. None: "I can't find the dentist on your calendar on Thursday."
12. **Remove, move, rename.** "Removed Dentist, Thursday at 3 PM." "Moved Dentist to Friday at 4 PM." "Renamed Dentist to Dentist check-up, Thursday at 3 PM." The times are said as adding says them. A move keeps the length unless an end is given, and an all-day event stays all day unless a time is given. A day alone keeps a timed event's time, and a time alone ("move the dentist to 5") keeps its day, which is the one found: Claude gives the time alone when only the time changes, so it never has to guess a day. A day already gone is refused, as adding does: "That day's already gone - which day did you mean?"
13. **A repeating event: that day's only, unless he says "all of them" or "every week".** Removing from this one on ends the series just before it, by its rule's `UNTIL` (a `COUNT` is replaced by the `UNTIL`, so the times before it are the series' own and nothing is counted), or deletes the whole series if it is the first. A day deleted on its own is a cancelled instance of its series, which Google drops once the rules end before it, and putting the rules back doesn't bring it back, so before any such cut or delete the cancelled instances from that time on are read (`instances` with `showDeleted`; if that can't be read, nothing is written) and the undo deletes each again. Moving or renaming from this one on ends the old series just before it and starts a new one from it with the same rule and the change, with only the times that were left: counting is only for that new series, whose `COUNT` is lowered by the times before it, one deleted on its own included, and which leaves out each day deleted on its own after the one it starts from with an `EXDATE` (the day at the new series' time of day, in UTC, for a timed series, a date for an all-day one). A series with guests is not moved or renamed from this one on: the new series is a copy without the guests, so cutting the old one would take their future times away. "That has guests. Change it in Google Calendar." is said and nothing is sent; that day's alone, or all of them from the first time on (which changes the series itself), keeps the guests as they are. Moving them all can change only the time of day: "I can only change the time for all of them. Move the days in Google Calendar." An occurrence which has already started is never changed, with or without "from this one on", and the same sentence is said; it counts as started when its start or the time it was meant to start at (`originalStartTime`) has passed, so a moved occurrence can't be used to reach back. A one-off event in the past can still be changed.
14. **Putting an event back** sets it to confirmed again, which brings back its reminders, repeats and guests; if that can't, a copy is added instead.
15. **"Undo that"** reverses the last playlist or calendar change, of any kind, within ten minutes: an add (the event or the song taken back off), a removal (the event put back, the song added again at the end, as Nova shuffles playlists anyway), a move or rename (the old times or name put back), a change from this one on (the old series' rule put back, the new series deleted, and each day deleted on its own after it deleted again - one which Google won't is said: "Put Gym back from Tuesday on, but Tuesday the 1st of December's time came back - remove it in Google Calendar."), a new playlist (deleted) and a like (unliked, and the other way). One change is kept, the latest, and one made of several playlists is undone whole; a part which failed is said. It is kept in memory only, so a restart forgets it, as the page's Undo does, and there is one slot for the whole house, held by the brain and shared by every satellite: "undo that" at either the PC or the Pi takes back the last change made at any of them, and something else done at either since leaves it alone, so "cancel the dentist" at one, "add milk" at the other and "undo that" at the first never puts the dentist back. An undo is sent once too, and said back: "Put Dentist back, Thursday at 3 PM." An undo can't itself be undone. Nothing to undo: "There's nothing to undo." Too late: "That was too long ago to undo."
16. **It only reaches a change which was the last thing Nova did.** Any other change since - milk on the shopping list, a timer - leaves it alone, so "undo that" never deletes the dentist when he meant the milk: "The last thing I did wasn't a playlist or calendar change, so I've left it." What only reads, such as asking what is on the calendar or the weather, doesn't count as something else: each tool says whether it changes anything (`changes`, true unless a tool says otherwise, which is the safe side, since a new tool nobody has declared can only block an undo, never undo the wrong thing). A change which failed, or which changed nothing ("Endless is already on Gym.", "Dentist was already gone."), leaves the slot as it was, and a change done on a yes is kept.
17. **An event already gone in Google** (deleted from his phone meanwhile) is said plainly when removing or undoing, and nothing else is sent.

**Approaches.**

For songs staying on his mirrored playlists:

1. **Stop SoulSync mirroring the three; later mirrors get the append switch for adding and a refusal for removing (chosen, his pick).** Adding and removing then always stay. The cost: the few songs still missing from the three still download into the library, but aren't put on their playlists.
2. Keep the mirrors and, on removing, also tell SoulSync to stop pairing the song with the playlist. The missing songs still arrive, but SoulSync then shows the song as missing, a manual sync might download it again, and it leans on more of SoulSync's insides.
3. Rejected: change Navidrome only (SoulSync undoes it), or change it on Spotify (he has left Spotify, and nothing refreshes the mirrors).

For the calendar there was one sensible way: Google's own `events.delete` and `events.patch` beside the `events.insert` Nova had.

**My calls, not the user's.** The designer's, his to change.

- Taking a song off a playlist removes every copy of it there.
- "The last song" is the one before the song at the queue's place.
- What counts as the same song, above.
- "Like this" and "add this to my liked songs" star the song, as "liked songs" already plays the starred ones.
- An invite from someone else is left alone.
- "Undo that" only reaches a change which was the last thing Nova did, lasts ten minutes, is kept in memory and is one slot for the whole house.
- A calendar or weather read between a change and "undo that" leaves the change undoable.
- An unset or unreachable SoulSync, a mirror it can't change and a mirror row it can't read each stop a change rather than guess.
- Nothing on the settings page.

**What isn't done.**

- Spotify playlists; deleting a whole playlist (other than undoing one just made); renaming a playlist; adding a song by name which isn't playing.
- Adding guests, changing an event's calendar, moving repeating events to other days, or changing past times of a repeating event.

**The one-off, his named job at deploy.** After `add-and-remove` is on GitHub's main, pulled as the Nova user and `nova-backend` restarted: read SoulSync's three mirrors (`GET /api/mirrored-playlists`), check each `server_playlist_id` is his Navidrome playlist, and `DELETE /api/mirrored-playlists/<id>` for each. That deletes only SoulSync's own rows for it; the Navidrome playlist is left as it is and never synced again. Then read Navidrome's playlists to confirm all six are still there. SoulSync's database is backed up nightly. Until this is done the three are still mirrors, and Nova treats them as such: it sets one to append before adding, and refuses to remove.

**Known limits.**

- Once the three stop being mirrors, the few songs still missing from them download into the library but aren't put on their playlists.
- A restart forgets the last change.
- Deleting days again after an undo of a cut, and the `EXDATE`s of a new series, are sent as Google's own `instances` and `recurrence` describe them but have not yet been tried on a real series (the live check of 19 Nov found the flaw; the check of the fix is in 34m and 34n). An `instances` read from the cut on of a series with no end ("every Tuesday" with no `UNTIL` or `COUNT`) pages as far as Google gives, and a change is not made if that is more than 20 pages.
- Putting a removed event back by setting it to confirmed has not yet been tried on a real event, nor on one occurrence of a repeating event; the copy is the fallback, and the live check on a throwaway event is still to do.
- With repeat-all on, the first song of the queue has no "last song", though the last one plays before it.

**Hardware.** None shapes this.
